# FX NEXT — Research Stack Architecture (rev 3)

Nov 6, 2026 · @Kobla · rev 3 (Nov 7, 2026) corrects rev 2, which corrected rev 1; both correction tables are listed first so nothing is silently changed.

## What this builds

A research pipeline that proves, out of sample and after the spread you will actually pay, which pairs and timeframes carry a directional edge — then trades the survivors as a portfolio, sized to a volatility target by calibrated probability. The TradingView indicator becomes a display layer; the learning moves to Python on years of data.

## Decisions

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pairs | 7 majors + 8 crosses: EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, USDCHF, USDCAD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD, EURGBP, EURJPY, GBPJPY, AUDJPY, EURAUD, EURCHF, CADJPY, AUDNZD | Breadth is the lever, but crosses are algebraic combinations of majors: 15 pairs hold 8 currencies and roughly 5–6 independent factors. NZDUSD, EURGBP, EURCHF and AUDNZD are expected to fail the cost screen below D1 (indicative 4h c = 0.082, 0.072, 0.10, 0.11 against the 0.06 threshold); of the ten tabulated pairs only EURUSD, USDJPY, GBPUSD, GBPJPY, AUDUSD and USDCHF are expected to pass at 4h. |
| Timeframes | 1h, 4h, D1 traded; 15m and 1m as feature sources | Where signal can clear spread (see cost table): 1h only for the four most liquid pairs against a 0.09 screen, 4h for six of the ten tabulated pairs, D1 for all. 15m cannot at retail spreads; 1m and 5m are further off. |
| Horizons | 4 and 8 bars per TF | Drift grows with H, noise with √H, and the cost share falls. |
| Target | Triple-barrier: +k·ATR, −k·ATR, time stop at H bars | Matches what a stop/target trade experiences |
| Model | Pooled LightGBM per TF + meta-model gate + calibration | Interactions, honest probabilities, and enough rows at 4h and D1 |
| Validation | Walk-forward, purged, embargoed, OANDA spread charged, CSCV over every config tried | The only numbers that count |
| Stack | Python 3.11+, polars, LightGBM, DuckDB + Parquet, Supabase, Vercel, GitHub Actions | Free tiers; a laptop is enough compute |
| Execution | Out of scope for v1; OANDA v20 when paper trading passes | Robinhood has no FX |

**Realistic outcome, done well.** A passing pair/TF: 55–57% hit rate on calls (the gate is ≥ 55%; sizing is zero below calibrated p = 0.56) at ≈12–20% coverage (≈15 calls/week at 1h, ≈4 at 4h, ≈1 at D1), net expectancy 0.02–0.07 ATR per call. Portfolio of survivors: Sharpe 0.2–1.0 after costs, central case 0.3–0.6, measured with a standard error of about 0.45 over the test window. Survivor count: 0–3 pair/TF combinations is the central expectation given the published record for developed currencies after costs; 6–10 is the good case. Most of 1h and several pairs will fail; that is the information this stack produces.

**What the published record says.** The numbers above are the optimistic case, not the central one. Daily technical rules on developed currencies: Hsu, Taylor and Wang test over 21,000 rules on 30 currencies over up to 40 years with a stepwise data-snooping test and find predictability in developed currencies mostly in the 1970s, a few rules in the 1980s and "none since the 1992–1996 period"; after a 0.025% one-way (0.05% round-trip) cost they find "almost no profitability in developed currencies" (JIE 2016). Neely and Weller's survey reaches the same conclusion: simple rules were genuinely profitable on major floating rates from about 1975 to 1990 and that profitability then disappeared. Cross-sectional 1-month momentum across up to 48 currencies earns a gross Sharpe of 0.95, but restricted to 15 developed currencies it is "basically non-existent after transaction costs" (Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling, Schrimpf, JFE 2012). The strongest credible machine-learning result is monthly, fundamentals-based and panel (ten developed currencies): Filippou, Rapach, Taylor and Zhou report a composite out-of-sample R² of 1.86%, gross Sharpe 0.80–0.97 over 1995–2020, falling to 0.53–0.73 at 75% of the bid-ask spread (JFQA 2026). Kılıç (Fed FEDS 2025-089) finds ridge-RFF market-timing Sharpe ratios "approaching 0.40" for eight majors that "collapse once an intercept is included", with the cross-currency mean out-of-sample R² bounded above by zero. Intraday: the one clean EURUSD classification number is 55% on a three-class target at a 200-second horizon (Fletcher and Shawe-Taylor, Comput. Econ. 2013), a horizon no retail spread survives; Dixon, Klabjan and Bang's pooled DNN on 43 CME futures including FX reports 42% ± 11% three-class accuracy at 5 minutes, with the density of per-instrument accuracies "around an accuracy of 0.35 which is slightly better than a random selection"; a 2026 walk-forward test of LSTM and gradient boosting on 5-minute index futures finds no configuration significantly above the 51.8% base rate. Equity intraday ML with ensembles does reach Sharpe 0.98 after costs (Huddleston, Liu, Stentoft, J. Fin. Econometrics 2023), but on the lagged returns of hundreds of index constituents, information FX does not have, and that predictability decreased post-decimalization. Calibration of this document's ranges against that record: a 55–57% hit rate on calls at 1h/4h/D1 after spread is optimistic (none of the developed-FX price-only results above clears costs at these horizons); the per-call IC is, for ±1-ATR barrier calls, the per-call net Sharpe, so it is the hit-rate claim restated and equally optimistic; a portfolio Sharpe of 0.4–1.0 after costs (the rev 2 range) brackets the best monthly fundamentals result (0.97 gross, 0.53–0.73 after costs) at its top and the entire developed-currency technical literature at its bottom, so 0.3–0.6 is the central range and 1.0 is the tail; 6–10 survivors of 90 configs is optimistic — under the null roughly 0.1–0.9 configs pass the single hit-rate or expectancy gate by chance, published base rates for this asset class and horizon are near zero, and survivors cluster by pair, so the central expectation is 0–3 pair/TF survivors, concentrated at D1 and 4h.

## The cost wall (why the timeframes changed)

Round-trip cost in ATR units is c = spread / ATR(14) at that timeframe. With symmetric k·ATR barriers, a barrier-hit fraction q (≈0.8 for k = 1, H = 4: a driftless walk with ATR(14)-scaled barriers gives 0.82; k = 1.5, H = 8 gives 0.77–0.80; spread-aware barriers and session seasonality shave a few points; the cost screen measures q from the store before use) and time-stop outcomes treated as zero drift:

```
net expectancy per call  E = q · k · (2p − 1) − c
break-even hit rate      p* = 0.5 + c / (2 · k · q)
```

Both lines are for p measured on mid-based barriers. The labels in this document are spread-aware (long: enter at ask, barriers on bid), so cost is already inside p: a no-skill model hits the upper barrier 0.5 − c/(2k) of the time (48% at c = 0.04, k = 1) with mean P&L ≈ −c, and break-even on resolved calls is ≈50% plus time-stop spread drag. Do not apply the E formula to a spread-aware p. The 55% hit-rate gate is on spread-aware labels (≈57–58% mid-based at 4h, c = 0.04); the hit-rate denominator is barrier-resolved calls, the expectancy denominator is all calls; q is reported per pair/TF and regime beside the hit rate.

**Cost screen (pre-gate, runs before any model):** a pair/TF enters the candidate set only if its median c over the test years is ≤ 0.06 at 4h and D1, or ≤ 0.09 at 1h (break-even 56.4% at k = 1), recomputed per pair, TF and session from the store. c is spread plus expected financing for that TF and horizon, direction-averaged (admin fee × expected nights, see below); P&L carries financing signed per trade. Combinations removed by the screen do not count toward the CSCV trial count: the screen uses no labels or performance and is fixed before modelling, so a screened combination has no out-of-sample P&L column to contribute.

**Financing.** OANDA applies financing to any position open at 17:00 New York: cost or credit = units × price at 17:00 × funding rate / 365, with separate long and short rates. The funding rate is the liquidity providers' tom-next rate adjusted by a 1.00% annualized admin fee against the client on each side (4.00% TRY, 2.00% CZK/HUF/SAR/THB/ZAR). Wednesday 17:00 is typically charged at three times the daily amount; Saturday and Sunday are not charged; holidays can add days. OANDA does not publish a historical funding-rate series through v20 (the instruments endpoint exposes only the current `financing.longRate` / `shortRate` and `financingDaysOfWeek`), so the backtest models it as (policy-rate differential from the BIS step function ∓ 1.00%), the scorer logs the current v20 rates daily, and the live account checks realized charges against DAILY_FINANCING transactions.

## System overview

Bars flow from OANDA into one Parquet store; Dukascopy cross-checks it; the calendar and macro feeds join as-of. A model reaches the scoring job only after the cost screen, the walk-forward gates and the portfolio simulation. The serving layer never sees one that did not pass.

From 2015 the store is ≈67M rows, 1.51× the 2019 count, so ≈3 GB of Parquet. Everything fits a 16 GB laptop.

**Candles.** Pull `price=BAM`, `granularity=M1`, `count` ≤ 5,000, `dailyAlignment=17`, `alignmentTimezone=America/New_York`, `weeklyAlignment=Friday`, `smooth=false`, `includeFirst=true`. Candle `time` is the candle start in UTC regardless of alignment timezone; the store converts to close-time by adding the granularity. Only candles with `complete == true` are stored; the live scorer drops the last candle if `complete` is false and never computes features from it. `volume` is the number of price updates in the candle, not units traded.

Minutes with no price update are expected to be absent from the response rather than returned as zero-volume candles (verify on first pull: request a two-hour weekday window fully inside trading hours on a thin pair, e.g. EURCHF 03:00–05:00 UTC on a Tuesday, and count returned candles against 120). The store keeps them absent and records `tick_count = 0` only when resampling to higher timeframes; the 1h/4h/D1 resampler counts sub-bars present, so `tick_count` for a 1h bar is the sum of returned 1m volumes and the number of missing minutes is a feature (`missing_1m`), not an error. QC distinguishes three kinds of missing 1m bar: scheduled (the daily 16:59–17:05 New York closure, the extra 6-minute break from 06:59 Auckland time on NZD pairs, weekend, holiday hours), no-tick (isolated minutes in thin sessions, bounded by a per-pair-session threshold learned from the first pull), and outage (runs longer than 15 minutes inside London or New York hours). Only the third blocks a pair.

Expected bars: 1,434 per full day (1,428 for NZDUSD and AUDNZD) minus no-tick minutes; a 4h bar has at most 240 1m bars; the first 4h bar of the week (Sunday 17:05–21:00 New York) has at most 235.

Time handling: source candles are stamped at bar open; the store stamps them at bar close. The FX day rolls at 17:00 America/New_York, which is 21:00 UTC in US summer and 22:00 UTC in winter; 4h and D1 bars align to that roll, matching OANDA's default alignment so backtest bars and live bars are the same bars. Weekends dropped. Mid price is the modelling series for price features; bid/ask is used for labels, P&L, the spread z-score feature (primary and meta-model), the cost screen and QC.

**Daily close.** OANDA forex is closed 16:59–17:05 New York every trading day (NZD pairs also 6 minutes from 06:59 Auckland). There are no 1m bars in that window. The week opens Sunday 17:05 and closes Friday 16:59 New York. The 17:00 close of 4h and D1 bars is inside the closure, so their close+5 min entry is the 17:05 reopening print. The cost screen reports c for the 17:05–17:15 window separately from the session median; it is expected to be several times the median (indicative, to be measured from the store: EURUSD 3–8 pips, GBPJPY 10–30 pips at reopen against 1.2 and 3.0 normally). D1 and the 17:00 4h call use a longer configured latency (17:20–17:30 New York, which needs a second scoring dispatch at 17:25 in addition to the hourly one) or are scored off the 21:00 bar; the choice is made from the measured spread curve, not assumed.

Calendar: OANDA forex closes 16:59 New York on 24 and 31 December and reopens 17:05 on 25 December and 1 January; US holidays (MLK, Presidents, Memorial, Juneteenth, 4 July when on a weekday, Labor Day, Thanksgiving) and Good Friday keep regular forex hours but are thin. The store carries a `thin_session` flag for 22 December–2 January, Good Friday through Easter Monday, and US/UK bank holidays; training rows and live calls on flagged days are excluded, and the exclusion is one function shared by backtest and scorer.

USD factor: a leave-one-out equal-weight basket of the 7 majors' USD returns (the pair being modelled is excluded so the feature is not its own return). If the ICE DXY is wanted it needs a USDSEK pull and reads:

```
DXY = 50.14348112 · EURUSD^-0.576 · USDJPY^0.136 · GBPUSD^-0.119 · USDCAD^0.091 · USDSEK^0.042 · USDCHF^0.036
```

QC report per pair before anything downstream: gap list (bars missing inside sessions, classified scheduled / no-tick / outage as above; the daily closure, the weekend Friday 16:59 → Sunday 17:05 and published holiday hours are whitelisted before anything is flagged), duplicate timestamps, spread outliers (> 10× session median), mid disagreement with Dukascopy, bars per day against the 1,434 expected, and the cost table above computed from the actual feed. A pair fails QC until the gap list is explained.

Every continuous feature is stored twice: raw, and as a rolling 250-bar right-aligned percentile rank. Rank versions survive vol-regime shifts; LightGBM picks whichever splits better. The ranks are causal and need no fold fitting. The 250-bar warm-up is about 2 weeks at 1h, about 8 weeks at 4h and one trading year at D1; it is served from bars before the training window rather than counted as missing. With a 2019 start the first complete D1 feature row is in 2020 and the first 24-month training window (test Q2 2021) has one year of rank features, which the > 1% missing rule would drop; pulling from 2015 (at minimum 2018) is therefore required for the stated fold schedule at D1, not optional. Features with more than 1% missing values, or a correlation above 0.97 with another, are dropped before training; the rules are label-free but use data, so they are applied inside each training window and the dropped list is stored with the model version, consistent with the global-standardization rule.

## Labels

The primary label is which barrier price touches first after the bar closes: +k·ATR (label +1), −k·ATR (−1), or the time stop at H bars (0, dropped from the direction model but kept for P&L). k = 1.0 ATR for 4-bar and 1.5 ATR for 8-bar horizons; both run and are compared. Expect 18–25% time stops; 25–35% would indicate ATR ≈ 1.85× the per-bar σ and should be investigated.

Barriers are spread-aware: a long enters at the ask and its barriers are checked against the bid; a short the reverse. Entry is the ask (long) or bid (short) of the first 1m bar whose close-time is ≥ bar close + `latency`, where `latency` is the measured scheduler delay (start at 5 minutes), not the closing mid; if that bar does not exist (closure, holiday, no ticks) the first later bar is used and `latency` is recorded as the actual delay. The same rule applies to the time-stop exit. A move that clears the barrier only on the mid is not a barrier hit: the window continues to the opposite barrier or the time stop.

**Gaps.** A barrier is 'touched' when a 1m bid (long) or ask (short) bar trades through it. The realized exit is the first tradable price at or beyond the barrier: the barrier itself when the 1m bar that crossed it opened on the near side, otherwise that bar's open. This applies to stops (worse than the barrier) and to take-profits (better than the barrier) symmetrically. When both barriers lie inside one 1m bar's range the stop is assumed to fill first.

Exposure: 4h H=8 (32 h) spans the weekend for entries from Thursday 09:05 New York onward, 8 of 30 weekly bar closes (27%); D1 H=4 spans it for 4 of 5 entry days (80%: entries at the Monday–Thursday closes, the Monday one because its time stop at Friday 17:05 falls inside the closure and exits at the Sunday reopen; the Friday close's entry is the Sunday 17:05 reopen and exits Thursday); D1 H=8 always. Weekend holds are not excluded; they are priced.

Gap frequency is measured from the store per pair as the share of weeks where |Sunday 17:05 open − Friday 16:59 close| exceeds 0.5 × D1 ATR(14) on the mid; the report carries this table. Indicative, to be replaced by the measurement: EURUSD median Sunday gap 2–5 pips, exceeding 0.5 ATR (≈35–40 pips at 2024–25 ATR) in roughly 1–2% of weeks; GBP and JPY crosses 2–4%. The tail comes from event gaps, weekend (French election April 2017, Brexit result overnight June 2016) and intraweek alike (SNB floor removal January 2015, GBP flash crash October 2016, JPY unwind August 2024); the same first-tradable-price rule prices all of them.

**Fills.** Orders are placed as a market order with `stopLossOnFill` and `takeProfitOnFill`, `timeInForce` FOK, and a `priceBound` of entry ± 0.1 ATR so a reopen or release print cannot fill an entry far from the signal price; a bound rejection is logged as a skipped call. The stop-loss uses trigger condition DEFAULT (OANDA compares a sell order to the bid and a buy order to the ask, so a long position's stop is compared to the bid and a short's to the ask) and is filled at the first price equal to or worse than the threshold, i.e. it behaves as a market order once triggered; the take-profit is filled at the first price equal to or better than the threshold, i.e. it behaves as a limit. The backtest realizes a take-profit at the barrier and a stop at the worse of the barrier and the close of the 1m bar that first traded through it; on a gap the first-tradable-price rule applies. The report tabulates realized stop slippage in pips by pair, TF, session and event flag.

Minimum distance: OANDA has no fixed minimum for ordinary stops or take-profits; both must be farther from the current price than the current spread or the order is rejected, and OANDA recommends placing them beyond the instrument's maximum spread. Guaranteed stop-loss orders (offered only by some OANDA entities) have a per-instrument minimum distance and a premium and are not used. A 1-ATR 1h stop (≈14 pips EURUSD, ≈28 USDJPY at the corrected ATR, ≈38 GBPJPY) clears the normal spread everywhere; at the 17:05 New York daily reopen and in the release minute, spreads on GBPJPY and the crosses can approach or exceed it, which is why entries are not placed there.

The v4 label (sign of the H-bar close-to-close move, minimum 0.3 ATR) is kept as a secondary target so Python can be checked against Pine on the same bars.

Overlapping samples: with H = 8 every bar's window overlaps those of 14 neighbours (7 before, 7 after) and at most 8 labels are open at any bar. Each sample is weighted by its average uniqueness: the mean over the bars in its window of 1/c_t, where c_t is the number of labels open at bar t. A 40-bar trend then carries total weight ≈ 40/8 = 5 rather than 40.

**Events.** Exclusion is by entry proximity, not by window content: no entry within 60 minutes before or 15 minutes after a scheduled high-impact release in either currency of the pair, in training and in the live scorer under the same function. Open positions ride through releases with their barriers in place, as they will live. Rows whose window contains a release are kept, flagged, and reported as a separate stratum (hit rate, expectancy, realized stop slippage with and without events) so the effect is measured rather than assumed away.

Why: the 12 historical series in the data layer give ≈32 releases a year for USD (8 FOMC, 12 NFP, 12 CPI), 20 for EUR and GBP, 8 for JPY, CAD and AUD, 7 for NZD and 4 for CHF, so a pair sees ≈1.0 high-impact releases per week (EURUSD, GBPUSD), ≈0.7 on average across the 15 pairs and ≈0.3 on the JPY/AUD/CAD/NZD crosses. Treating arrivals as Poisson, the probability that a window contains none is, for EURUSD / the average pair: ≈0.94 / 0.96 (1h H=8), 0.88 / 0.91 (4h H=4), 0.77 / 0.83 (4h H=8), 0.45 / 0.58 (D1 H=4), 0.20 / 0.34 (D1 H=8). Whole-window exclusion would therefore leave ≈65 (EURUSD) to ≈110 (average pair) D1 H=8 rows per pair per 24-month window out of ≈320; no model fits that, and the live scorer would suppress ≈65–80% of D1 H=8 calls on the USD, EUR and GBP pairs. The exact counts come from the agency schedules in the store. Stop fills during the release minute are priced by the gap rule above (first tradable price); the report tabulates the share of stops that filled worse than the barrier and by how much, by pair, TF and event/no-event.

## Models

Three models in sequence per timeframe: a direction model, a gate that predicts whether the direction call will be right, and a calibrator that turns scores into probabilities you can size on.

The 3-month validation fold serves one purpose: early stopping of the primary. Out-of-fold primary scores for the meta-model come from purged 4-fold cross-fitting inside the 24-month training window (tree count fixed from early stopping; purge 8 bars + entry latency and embargo 1 day between folds); the primary is then refit on the full window. The calibrator is fit on cross-fitted meta scores, never on the meta-model's own in-sample outputs. A 3-month fold pooled over 15 pairs holds ≈750–800 4h calls and ≈170–260 D1 calls — too few for a 10-feature meta-model and below the ≥ 1,000 isotonic rule once pairs are screened; cross-fitting yields ≈28,000 (4h) and ≈4,800 (D1) rows of OOF scores. Otherwise the meta-model and calibrator learn the primary model's in-sample overconfidence. The hit-rate feature uses only outcomes resolved before bar t.

Ensembling is across targets, not seeds: a call is emitted when the 4-bar (k = 1.0) and 8-bar (k = 1.5) direction models agree and the last completed bar of the next timeframe's model (1h → 4h, 4h → D1; D1 has no parent and is unfiltered) has not called the opposite direction; a parent 'none' does not block. The traded barrier set is fixed before the test folds (default: the 4-bar k = 1.0 set), since the two horizons' P&L differ. The agreement filter is a configuration: it counts in the CSCV trial count, and coverage, N ≥ 2,000, hit rate, Brier and net expectancy are all computed on the post-filter call set, which is roughly half the single-horizon call count. Agreement counts are also meta-model inputs.

Retrain cadence is the procedure the walk-forward validated: the primary is refit quarterly on the trailing 24 months with a 3-month validation fold, and the meta-model and calibrator are refit at the same cadence on the same object as in the backtest, the cross-fitted scores of the current primary vintage — not weekly on 6 months spanning several vintages, which the backtest never tested. Models are versioned by training-window end date and stored with their feature list, dropped-feature list and validation report.

## Validation protocol and go/no-go gates

Every number that matters comes from walk-forward out-of-sample folds with OANDA spread charged; nothing from the training window is ever reported. Train 24 months, validate 3 (early stopping of the primary only; the meta-model and calibrator are cross-fitted inside the training window, see Models), test 3, roll forward one quarter at a time. With data from 2019 the first test quarter is Q2 2021 and there are 22 test quarters through Q3 2026. Pulling from 2015 adds 16 test quarters, Q2 2017 → Q1 2021 (38 in all), as a secondary out-of-time check.

Purge at every boundary between a fitted segment and the segment that evaluates it (train→validation, validation→test): drop the last H_max = 8 bars plus the entry latency of the earlier segment, so no label in the earlier segment resolves inside the later one, then embargo one day. Purging only between train and test would leave validation labels that resolve in the test quarter, and early stopping, the meta-model and the calibrator would then be selected on test-period outcomes. Rolling features that reach back across a boundary are not leakage in forward-only walk-forward. Test: max(label_end_ts of earlier segment) < min(entry_ts of later segment) for every fold and boundary. Reported metrics are pooled across all test quarters, by pair/TF and by regime, session and quarter. Per-fold numbers are too noisy to gate on: a 3-month 4h test fold has ≈50 calls per pair at the stated call rate.

| Gate | Pass threshold | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Cost screen | median c = (spread + expected financing) / ATR ≤ 0.06 at 4h and D1, ≤ 0.09 at 1h | Break-even hit rate ≤ 54.3% at k = 1 (q = 0.7) at 0.06, 56.4% at 0.09. A 55% model at c = 0.06 nets ≈ 0.01 ATR and turns negative above c = 0.07 (a 17% spread widening); at a 50% widening (c = 0.09) break-even is 56.4%. Robustness to spread widening is enforced by the net-expectancy gate (gross ≥ 1.5 × cost), not by this screen. |
| Hit rate on calls | ≥ 55% on N ≥ 2,000 barrier-resolved calls pooled across the pairs that passed the cost screen at that TF; calendar-block-bootstrap 99% interval excludes 50% | N ≥ 2,000 makes the rate itself stable to about ±1 pt. Per pair over 22 test quarters at the stated call rates: 1h ≈4,300 calls, 4h ≈1,150, D1 ≈290, of which ≈80% are barrier-resolved, so a per-pair N ≥ 2,000 gate would exclude D1 and all of 4h by construction. Pooled over the pairs expected to pass the screen: 1h (4 pairs) ≈17,000 calls, 4h (6 pairs) ≈6,900, D1 (all 15) ≈4,300; D1 reaches 2,000 resolved calls only with ≥ 9 pairs in the pool over 22 quarters, or ≥ 5 over the 38 quarters a 2015 pull gives. Per-pair hit rates are reported with block-bootstrap intervals and inform inclusion through the pooled model's per-pair breakdown. Overlapping windows make calls dependent, so a binomial test overstates significance; the block bootstrap does not. |
| Brier score (calls) | report; gate on Brier skill vs the constant forecast p̄ > 0 with the calendar-block-bootstrap 99% interval excluding 0 | A calibrated model scores p̄(1−p̄) − Var(p): p̄ = 0.55 → 0.2475, 0.56 → 0.2464, 0.57 → 0.2451, 0.58 → 0.2436; coin flip 0.250. A raw gate at < 0.245 would require calibrated p̄ ≥ 0.571 (≈0.565 with typical dispersion) and fail the 55–57% models the hit-rate gate admits. Calibration itself is tested by the slope gate. |
| Calibration slope | 0.8–1.2, reliability curve monotone | Sizing on p requires p to mean what it says |
| Net expectancy per call | > 0 and ≥ 0.5 × round-trip cost per pair/TF, reported with its calendar-block-bootstrap 90% interval; significance (t ≥ 2, block bootstrap) on the pooled per-TF call set, not per pair/TF | Realistic passing values are 0.02–0.07 ATR. Gross edge at least 1.5× cost means a 50% wider spread leaves it non-negative (exactly zero at the gate boundary). With per-call P&L sd ≈ 0.9 ATR, t = E·√N/0.9: t ≥ 3 needs E ≥ 0.06 ATR at N = 2,000 and 0.04 at N = 5,000, so a 55% model at 4h (E ≈ 0.03 ATR, ≈1,150 calls per pair over 22 quarters, t ≈ 1.1 even under independence) could never pass per pair, and D1 (≈290 calls) at no E in the 0.02–0.07 range. Pooled, the same model reaches t ≈ 2.8 at 4h (≈6,900 calls) and ≈2.2 at D1 (≈4,300), which is what the pooled t ≥ 2 rule is sized for. |
| Stability | ≥ 60% of test quarters positive; no single quarter > 30% of total | A Sharpe-1.0 strategy has 69% positive quarters in expectation, ±10 pts over 22 quarters; 70% would reject it half the time; 60% passes it 79% of the time (Sharpe 0.7: 60%, Sharpe 0.5: 45%). No quarterly t-stat gate: over 22 quarters t = SR_annual × 2.35, so t ≥ 2.5 would require SR ≥ 1.07 per pair/TF — above the whole 0.2–1.0 range expected for the portfolio — and reject every model this document expects to find (expected t: 1.2 at SR 0.5, 1.6 at SR 0.7). Significance comes from the expectancy bootstrap gate and the portfolio decision rule. |
| Shuffle test | Full pipeline (uniqueness weights, meta-model, calibrator, hit-rate feature) trained on randomly permuted labels; out-of-sample AUC within 0.50 ± 3·SE, SE ≈ √(1/(3N)) for N test rows (per pair ≈ ±0.013 at 1h, ±0.025 at 4h, ±0.06 at D1) | A fixed ±0.02 is ~1 SE at D1 and fails clean pairs about one time in three. It detects label leakage through the pipeline (train/test row duplication, label-derived features using unresolved outcomes, test labels reaching the calibrator). It does not detect lookahead in price features: permuting labels severs the feature–label link, so a feature built from future bars still scores 0.50. Feature lookahead is caught by the truncation test (recompute each feature at t from data truncated at t; assert equality) and a forward-shift test (shift features one bar forward; AUC must jump). It does not catch purge failures; the timestamp-overlap test does. |
| Overfit check | Probability of backtest overfitting (CSCV) < 0.2 across all configs backtested, one number for the project, not a per-pair/TF gate (the paper's illustrative cutoff is 0.05; 0.2 is a project convention); report the Deflated Sharpe Ratio with the effective number of independent trials (clustered by return correlation), the variance of trial Sharpe ratios, sample length, skewness and kurtosis | Every backtested configuration counts, including discarded ones: pair × TF × {k, H} × hyper-parameter set × meta threshold × ensemble rule. Combinations removed by the cost screen do not count: CSCV is computed on the matrix of out-of-sample P&L series of configurations actually backtested, and a screened combination has no column to contribute. |

A pair/TF that passes every gate enters the portfolio. One that fails is reported, not tuned until it passes. The configuration count is logged from day one so the CSCV test is honest.

Block bootstrap, for the hit-rate interval, the Brier skill interval and the expectancy t: moving-block bootstrap over calendar blocks, block length ≥ 2·H bars and never shorter than the label window: 1 week at 1h and 4h; 4 weeks (20 trading days) at D1, where an 8-bar label spans 8 trading days and depends on 7 neighbours either side (15 bars, about 3 weeks), so a 5-day block would be shorter than one label and understate the variance. Blocks are indexed on the calendar and shared across pairs so pooled per-TF and portfolio statistics keep same-week cross-pair dependence. ≥ 1,000 resamples.

What the test window can and cannot tell you: over 5.5 years the standard error of an annualized Sharpe estimate is √((1 + SR²/(2m))/T), m observations per year and T in years (Lo 2002): ≈0.43 at SR = 1 on daily or monthly P&L, ≈0.45 on quarterly; call it ≈0.45. (√((1 + SR²/2)/5.5) = 0.52 holds only for annual observations.) The gates can establish that an edge is not zero. They cannot distinguish Sharpe 1.0 from Sharpe 0.5, and the plan below does not pretend they can.

## Portfolio and sizing

The gain lives here, not in the forecaster: the same weak signal spread across every pair/TF that passed its gate, sized to a vol target by calibrated probability, with currency exposure capped.

Expected scaling follows the fundamental law of active management, IR ≈ IC · √BR · TC. IC here is the per-call Sharpe after spread, E/σ from the cost-wall formula: with q = 0.7, k = 1 and c = 0.04, E = 0.7·(2p − 1) − 0.04 and σ ≈ 0.83 ATR, so IC ≈ 0.02 at p = 0.54, 0.036 at 0.55, 0.053 at 0.56 and 0.07 at 0.57. BR is the number of *independent* bets per year, not the number of calls. Nominal call count for 10 survivors (4 at 1h, 4 at 4h, 2 at D1; ≈15 calls/week per 1h model, ≈4 per 4h, ≈1 per D1, i.e. ≈12–20% coverage) is ≈4,000 a year; same-pair calls across horizons and timeframes, and concurrent positions sharing the USD factor, cut the independent count to ≈500–1,000 (a prior; the portfolio simulation measures it, see below). The improvement over one pair on one timeframe is in √BR, so a 10× bet count is a ≈3× IR, not 10×. TC 0.5–0.6 is a conservative prior for a long-short book whose only constraints are the currency-exposure cap and the position cap. All three terms are already net of spread, so:

```
IR ≈ 0.02 · √500 · 0.5 ≈ 0.2      to      0.07 · √1000 · 0.6 ≈ 1.3    (after costs)
```

The central case (p ≈ 0.55, BR ≈ 700, TC ≈ 0.55) is ≈0.5, which is why the realistic-outcome line quotes 0.3–0.6 as the central band and 1.0 as the tail; the lower tail is ≈0.2, which is why the decision rule below stops at Sharpe < 0.3.

Breadth and transfer are measured in the portfolio simulation, not assumed. BR_eff is reported two ways and the smaller is used: (a) from the realized average correlation ρ̄ of concurrent position P&L, BR_eff ≈ calls / (1 + (n̄ − 1)·ρ̄) with n̄ the average open count — at n̄ ≈ 7 and ρ̄ ≈ 0.3, the sizing assumptions below, that is 4,000 / 2.8 ≈ 1,400, above the top of the prior range, which is one reason the smaller of the two measures is used; (b) as the effective dimensionality of the daily P&L correlation matrix across pair/TF models (number of eigenvalues ≥ 1), following Polakow and Gebbie. Horizons add nothing to either count: a call already requires the 4-bar and 8-bar models to agree, so a pair/TF produces one bet per entry. Transfer coefficient: positions are in ATR units with weight shape (2p − 1 − c)/k, so weights are proportional to the forecast and the vol target, a uniform scalar on every weight, leaves TC untouched. TC falls below 1.0 only through the currency-exposure cap, calls skipped at the position cap, the p = 0.56 floor, and calibration error in p. A reasonable prior for such a book is 0.7–0.9; the 0.5–0.6 above is deliberately conservative and is replaced by the measured corr(position weight, (2p − 1 − c)/k) across all calls in the test folds, skipped calls entering at zero weight.

Sizing per call: Kelly after cost on even barriers with barrier-hit fraction q and time stops at zero drift is f ≈ (2p − 1)/k − c/(q·k²); the simpler (2p − 1 − c)/k is the q = 1 case and remains the weight shape. A quarter of f is the position's relative weight. At p = 0.56, c = 0.04, k = 1, q = 0.7–0.8 the growth-optimal fraction is ≈0.06–0.07 (×¼ ≈ 1.5–1.7% of equity); at p = 0.62 ≈ 0.17–0.19 (×¼ ≈ 4.3–4.7%), so without a level control a 1% cap binds on nearly every call and sizing is flat. The level is therefore set by the vol target, not by Kelly:

| Rule | Setting | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio vol target | 10% annualized realized vol | Primary control. |
| Risk per position | derived: ≈0.1–0.2% of equity to the stop | ≈4,000 trades/year, per-trade P&L std ≈ 0.9 × risk, ≈7 concurrent positions at average pairwise correlation ≈ 0.3 → annual vol ≈ 0.9·√(4,000 × 2.8) ≈ 95 × risk → 10% vol at ≈0.11% (≈0.18% if positions were uncorrelated, vol ≈ 57 × risk). 0.3% would run 17–29% vol. The 1% cap from rev 1 (57–95% vol) would never bind and is dropped. |
| Shape within the level | quarter-Kelly-after-cost, zero below calibrated p = 0.56 | The selectivity dial made continuous |
| Per-currency exposure | net exposure to any currency ≤ 3× a single position's risk | Mechanical cap on the USD factor bet |
| Open positions | cap 12; expected average load ≈ 7; calls arriving at the cap are skipped and logged, never queued | Arrival rate × holding time: 60 × 5h + 16 × 20h + 2 × 120h per 120h week ≈ 7 open |
| Kill switch | CUSUM of per-call Brier excess over 0.25, reset at zero, threshold set on the test folds for ≤ 1 false halt per model-year; a halt stops that model until retrain | A fixed 20-day window has ≈16 calls at 4h at the stated call rate and trips on noise |

**Account jurisdiction.** OANDA Corporation (US) accounts are subject to NFA Compliance Rule 2-43(b): offsetting positions in the same instrument are not carried but are offset first-in-first-out, hedging is not available (except on ECP accounts), and since 16 Aug 2019 OANDA additionally cancels a second open trade in the same pair with the same size when either trade carries a stop-loss, take-profit or trailing stop (two same-pair same-size trades are allowed only if neither has one). Opposite calls in one pair from different timeframes therefore close the older position rather than hedge it, and same-pair stacking needs distinct unit counts. The portfolio simulation applies the same rules when `account.hedging = false`: a new opposite call nets against the oldest open trade in that pair (realizing its P&L at the current bid/ask and dropping its barriers) and same-pair same-direction calls are merged into one position whose barriers are the newest call's. Non-US OANDA entities (e.g. Europe, Global Markets, Asia Pacific, Australia, Canada) carry opposite trades and per-trade barriers and are simulated as written. The setting is a config value; the live scorer reads `hedgingEnabled` from the v20 account summary and refuses to run if it does not match the configured value.

**Units and margin.** Units = risk × equity / (stop distance × pip value per unit); the minimum order is 1 unit, so 0.1% of a $1,000 account against a 14-pip stop is 714 units and placeable. On $10,000 at 1 ATR stops: 1h (14 pips) 7,100 units at 0.1%, 21,400 at 0.3%; 4h (30 pips) 3,300 / 10,000; D1 (70 pips) 1,400 / 4,300.

Margin: US retail (CFTC 17 CFR 5.9) 2% of notional on major-currency pairs, 5% otherwise; all 15 pairs here are 2% (50:1). EU/UK retail (ESMA Decision 2018/796, carried into national rules) 3.33% (30:1) on pairs made of two of USD, EUR, JPY, GBP, CAD, CHF — EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, USDCHF, USDCAD, EURGBP, EURJPY, GBPJPY, EURCHF, CADJPY — and 5% (20:1) on AUDUSD, NZDUSD, AUDJPY, EURAUD, AUDNZD; margin close-out when account equity falls below 50% of total initial margin.

Margin as a share of equity per position (pair quoted in the account currency) = risk × price / (stop pips × pip) × margin rate. EURUSD at 1.16: 1h 0.1% → 1.7% (US) / 2.8% (EU); 1h 0.3% → 5.0% / 8.3%; 4h 0.3% → 2.3% / 3.9%; D1 0.3% → 1.0% / 1.7%. Twelve concurrent 1h positions at 0.3% → 60% (US) / 99% (EU) of equity; at 0.1% → 20% / 33%. The portfolio simulation enforces a margin-usage cap of 40% of equity on top of the position cap; calls that would breach it are skipped and logged with reason `margin`. At the 12-position cap that admits 1h risk of ≈0.2% per position on a US account and ≈0.12% on an EU account; at the expected load of ≈7 positions, ≈0.34% and ≈0.2%. On an EU account the usable 1h risk range is therefore ≈0.1–0.2%, and at 0.3% the margin cap, not the position cap or the kill switch, would be the binding control.

The portfolio simulation runs on the same walk-forward test folds with the same spread and financing model, the same entry latency, the same fill and gap rules and the account's netting rule, so its Sharpe is comparable to the per-model gates. Decision rule, with the ≈0.45 standard error in mind:

- Sharpe < 0.3 after costs, or fewer than 3 survivors: stop.
- 0.3–0.7: continue research; no paper trading.
- ≥ 0.7 with ≥ 3 survivors and a lower 1-SE bound above 0.2: paper trade.

## Serving

A GitHub Actions job runs hourly during FX hours (the traded timeframes are 1h, 4h and D1, so hourly is sufficient): pull the latest bars from OANDA, compute features, score every live model, write calls to Supabase, and let a database webhook push the ones with size > 0 to your phone. Supabase cannot run LightGBM, so Python runs in the Action and Supabase is the system of record.

Scheduling facts that shape this: GitHub documents `schedule` as best-effort: runs 'can be delayed during periods of high load', the start of every hour is a named high-load period, 'some queued jobs may be dropped', and the shortest interval is 5 minutes. No GitHub figure backs a delay range; community reports include multi-hour delays. In a public repo `schedule` is auto-disabled after 60 days without repository activity. GitHub Free (and Free for organizations) includes $12 of Actions compute per month for private repos, stated in money, not minutes: ≈2,000 min on the standard 2-core Linux runner ($0.006/min) or ≈6,000 min on the 1-core ubuntu-slim runner ($0.002/min); each job is rounded up to a whole minute. Hourly over FX hours is ≈520 runs/month; at ≈2 min a run that is ≈1,040 min ≈ $6.24 (2-core) or ≈$2.08 (ubuntu-slim), under the allowance. The ≈2 min per run is an estimate to be measured in the first week: runner start + checkout, restore of a cached environment (actions/cache, 10 GB per repo, entries unused for 7 days evicted; the hourly run keeps it warm), then fetch + features + scoring; the private-repo runner is 2 vCPU / 8 GB (ubuntu-slim: 1 vCPU / 5 GB). A 15-minute cadence is ≈2,080 runs ≈ 4,160 min ≈ $25 on the 2-core runner (over) but ≈$8 on ubuntu-slim (under): hourly is chosen because it is sufficient, not because 15-min is unaffordable. Standard runners are free in public repos. Trigger from an external free cron (e.g. cron-job.org: free, down to 1-minute intervals, custom method/headers/body) with POST /repos/{owner}/{repo}/actions/workflows/{workflow_id}/dispatches, body {"ref":"main"}, authenticated with a fine-grained PAT holding the repository 'Actions: write' permission; the workflow file must exist on the default branch, and once it has run at least once it can be dispatched against any branch or tag (Vercel Hobby cron cannot do this: once per day, ±59 min precision). `workflow_dispatch` bypasses the scheduler, but the run is still queued for a runner (avoid firing exactly at :00), so the delay from bar close to scoring is measured and logged, not assumed; that delay is the `latency` the backtest charges.

PSI breaches (feature or score) open a retrain review; only the Brier CUSUM sets status = halted. Retraining happens only on the fixed quarterly schedule (retrain.yml); a PSI or CUSUM breach never triggers an unscheduled retrain.

The TradingView indicator stays as the on-chart view. v4 already works standalone; a v5 variant can take a pasted weight vector from the Python logistic baseline so the chart shows the same call the stack made.

## Build plan

Each phase ends at a gate; a gate that fails stops the plan rather than loosening the gate. Honest effort: ingest and QC ≈ 12 h, features ≈ 12 h, labels and weights ≈ 6 h, walk-forward, gates, bootstrap and CSCV ≈ 18 h, meta and calibration ≈ 6 h, portfolio simulation ≈ 10 h, serving and alerts ≈ 12 h, dashboard ≈ 8 h, leakage tests ≈ 8 h, plus debugging. That is 100–120 hours: 10–12 weeks at 10 h/week, or 5–6 at 20. Then 8 weeks of paper trading, which at ≈80 calls a week pooled gives ≈640 calls, enough to detect a 5-pt hit-rate shortfall at about 2.5 standard errors.

Kill criteria, decided now so they are not renegotiated later:

- After the cost screen: fewer than 6 pair/TF combinations survive at 1h and 4h combined (D1 passes for every tabulated pair by construction) → the edge, if any, is at D1 only; evaluate D1 alone and stop if it fails.
- After eval: no pair/TF passes every validation gate → stop; the edge is not at these horizons.
- After the portfolio simulation: Sharpe below 0.3 after costs, or fewer than 3 survivors → stop; breadth did not rescue a thin signal.
- Paper trade: pooled live hit rate more than 2 standard errors below out-of-sample on ≥ 300 calls → halt and audit for leakage. Per-model comparisons at this sample size are noise and are reported, not acted on.

Cost: zero in cash if the repo is private and under the $12/month included Actions compute (≈2,000 min on the 2-core runner, ≈6,000 on ubuntu-slim; hourly scoring is ≈$2–6), or public. OANDA practice, Supabase, Vercel Hobby, ntfy and an external cron are free at this scale; the data is ≈2 GB from 2019, ≈3 GB from 2015 (Parquet estimates). Time is the only budget.

## Leakage and realism checklist

Each item is a unit test in `tests/`, run on every build. A backtest showing 60%+ on 4h FX direction, or anything at all on 15m after spread, has failed one of these.

- [ ] Every rolling statistic is right-aligned: the value at bar t uses bars ≤ t only
- [ ] Rolling percentile ranks are causal by construction; any *global* standardization (a fixed mean/std or a quantile map) is fitted inside the training fold only and applied to test rows
- [ ] Higher-timeframe features come from the last HTF bar that closed before bar t, never the one containing it
- [ ] Daily macro values are joined as-of their FRED/BIS publication time, not their observation date: DGS2 for day T appears on FRED on the afternoon of T+1 (observed 15:16–16:05 CST, ≈21:00–22:00 UTC), VIXCLS for day T on the morning of T+1 (observed 08:38 CST, ≈14:40 UTC); BIS CBPOL is refreshed weekly (on 6 Nov 2026 the latest US/XM/JP/CH observation was 3 Nov, AU 29 Oct). Backtest rows see exactly what the live scorer could have downloaded at scoring time. Policy-rate steps are timestamped from the central-bank announcement; BIS is backfill only
- [ ] Event timestamps come from the issuing agency's schedule, are converted to UTC from the agency's own timezone, and are checked against a known NFP release (BLS schedule date, usually but not always the first Friday — e.g. 8 Jan 2027 and 8 Oct 2027 are second Fridays — at 08:30 America/New_York)
- [ ] Session flags and the daily roll use America/New_York and Europe/London local time, and a test covers one week in each window where US and UK DST differ (March, and late October/early November)
- [ ] Resampled bar timestamps are the bar close; features at bar t cannot see bar t+1's open
- [ ] Labels use bid for long exits and ask for short exits; the barrier check never uses mid; a stop realizes the worse of the barrier and the crossing 1m bar's close, a take-profit the barrier, and a gap the first tradable price
- [ ] Label P&L includes signed financing from the roll timestamps the trade spanned; `financing_pips` is asserted against the expected-nights table
- [ ] Entry is at the ask/bid of the 1m bar at close + configured latency, never the closing mid; the configured latency is ≥ the measured live median
- [ ] Purge (H_max = 8 bars + entry latency) and embargo are applied at every train→validation and validation→test boundary and asserted with a timestamp-overlap test: max(label_end_ts of earlier segment) < min(entry_ts of later segment)
- [ ] Meta-model and calibrator are trained only on cross-fitted out-of-fold primary scores; a test asserts no in-sample score reaches them
- [ ] The rolling hit-rate feature uses only outcomes whose resolution timestamp is < t
- [ ] The event exclusion rule (entry proximity: 60 min before, 15 min after) and the `thin_session` exclusion in training are the same functions the live scorer uses to suppress calls
- [ ] Shuffle test: the full pipeline trained on permuted labels yields out-of-sample AUC within 0.50 ± 3·SE, SE ≈ √(1/(3N)), on every pair/TF
- [ ] Truncation test: each feature at t recomputed from data truncated at t equals the stored value; forward-shift test: features shifted one bar forward make AUC jump
- [ ] Configuration count is logged for the CSCV test; every backtested config counts, discarded ones included; cost-screened combinations, which were never backtested, do not
- [ ] Live scoring and backtest scoring share one feature function and one label function, not two implementations
- [ ] Spread used in P&L is the actual OANDA bid/ask at entry and exit, not a flat assumption and not Dukascopy's
- [ ] Skipped calls (position cap, margin cap, event window, thin session, price bound, halted model) are logged and included in coverage statistics
- [ ] The live scorer refuses to run if the account's `hedgingEnabled` does not match the configured netting rule, and drops any candle with `complete == false`
